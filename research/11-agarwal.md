# UX inspiration review: Agarwal Packers & Movers Delhi

**Reference inspected:** [Delhi service page](https://www.agarwalpackers.com/packersmoversdelhi.html), with the linked [Contact page](https://www.agarwalpackers.com/contact.html) checked for the handoff after a user chooses to contact the company.

## Summary

The reference is a content-heavy, trust-led local landing page. It helps a visitor self-identify a move type, understand the process, and validate the company through scale, tracking, offices, certifications, and anti-fraud guidance. Conversion is deliberately phone-first: the Delhi page makes the large national number and “instant moving quote” callout prominent, but does not expose a short quote form in the main page content. The overall pattern is useful for Perfect Packways, provided the same breadth is reorganized into a shorter, more task-oriented flow.

## Strengths

1. **Clear service discovery by intent.** The page distinguishes household, office, intercity, vehicle, international, and storage needs, then adds route and local-area links. This gives both broad and location-specific entry points rather than forcing every visitor through one generic service description. [1]

2. **Strong trust architecture.** Experience, branch/service-point counts, country coverage, GPS-enabled vehicles, storage capacity, trained workers, certification imagery, a named branch manager, and an official address appear across the page. The “Original” guidance is especially relevant in a category where look-alike brands create uncertainty. [1]

3. **The moving process is made legible.** A six-step sequence—from sharing move details through assessment, quotation, packing, transport, and delivery—sets expectations and explains why a price is not shown as a single fixed number. [1]

4. **Multiple reassurance and post-booking paths.** Consignment tracking, FAQ, customer-care links, damage/claims support, feedback, and office/contact details extend beyond lead capture. The contact page also separates booking, tracking, claims, complaints, and feedback destinations. [2]

## Weaknesses and friction

1. **The primary quote path is underspecified.** The main Delhi page asks users to call for an “instant moving quote,” but the visible content does not present a short form or show the minimum information needed to start asynchronously. Users who cannot call must discover the separate Contact page or other links. [1] [2]

2. **Information architecture is very deep and repetitive.** The page combines global navigation, a large international-location directory, a long Delhi article, route/local-area links, service portfolio, FAQs, and a very large footer. This creates useful SEO coverage but makes the decision path feel long and increases scanning cost. [1]

3. **Visual hierarchy is diluted by link density and competing utilities.** Online payment, customer care, process, trucking, CSR, FAQ, fake-brand warning, USPs, worldwide presence, tracking, country selection, and services all compete near the top-level navigation. A visitor seeking a quote has to distinguish operational/support tools from the primary acquisition action. [1]

4. **Responsive behavior is only partially evident from the inspected render.** The rendered desktop view exposes a hamburger control, but the page was not fully interaction-tested at a narrow mobile viewport. The page’s very long content, repeated location lists, and dense navigation suggest that mobile users would benefit from collapsed sections, sticky “Get estimate”/call actions, and shorter progressive disclosure rather than simply stacking the desktop page. [1]

## Original ideas Perfect Packways could adapt

1. **A two-lane estimate entry point:** place “Get a quick estimate” beside “Call now,” then ask only origin, destination, move date, property type, and phone. Offer a later inventory/video survey rather than forcing a long first form.

2. **A move-type decision grid with tailored proof:** let users choose Home, Office, Vehicle, International, or Storage. Change the next screen’s proof points and checklist to match that intent, instead of presenting every service and industry at once.

3. **A compact trust strip that is verifiable:** show a few honest metrics, named local operating area, insurance/claims policy, tracking method, and review count. Each item should open a small detail panel or evidence page so trust is earned without overwhelming the landing page.

4. **A “what your estimate includes” receipt:** before submission, summarize packing, loading, transport, unloading, unpacking, access fees, and optional services as included/not included. This adapts the reference’s advice to understand quote scope while reducing later surprise and support contacts.
