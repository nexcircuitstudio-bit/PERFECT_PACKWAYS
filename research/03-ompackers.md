# UX research: Om Packers & Movers — North Goa landing page

**Reference inspected:** [North Goa Packers and Movers](https://ompackersindia.com/north-goa-packers-movers-goa)

The page is a location-specific lead-generation landing page. Its information architecture moves from utility links and contact details, to a location headline, then an above-the-fold quote form, service explanation, price guidance, service-specific links, trust proof, reviews, FAQs, and repeated quote prompts. The rendered desktop view also shows a persistent “Get Quote within 10 mints.” WhatsApp CTA; the header collapses to a menu button in the responsive presentation.

## Strengths

1. **Quote capture is visible early and asks for decision-relevant inputs.** The hero form collects origin, destination, phone, move timing, move type, and service category. This is a better qualification pattern than a generic “Contact us” form because it matches how a moving estimate is actually scoped. [1]

2. **Service discovery is broad and locally contextualized.** The page links to home shifting, car transportation, office shifting, loading/unloading, packing/unpacking, insurance, claims, and IBA-related pages, all framed around North Goa. This supports both users who know their service and users who are still exploring. [1]

3. **Trust is repeated in several formats.** The page surfaces IBA approval, government authorization, ISO certification, years in service, a registered-trademark claim, ratings, reviews, galleries, an app link, and a fraud-warning link. The variety helps address different objections: legitimacy, safety, experience, and social proof. [1]

4. **Price guidance reduces uncertainty before contact.** Rate bands by home size, distance, car, and bike give visitors a useful planning baseline, while the note says the final cost varies with equipment, road conditions, and delivery timing. [1]

5. **The site offers multiple contact modes.** The page exposes phone contact, quote forms, a WhatsApp shortcut, branch selection, and a separate quote/search destination. This is useful for users with different levels of urgency and comfort. [1] [2]

## Weaknesses and friction

1. **The page is very long and repetitive.** The same service keywords, “Request a Quote” actions, and promotional claims recur across a large content stack. This creates scroll fatigue and makes it harder to understand the essential next step. The browser-rendered document is roughly 9,200px tall and contains 46 images, which also raises a performance and bandwidth concern on mobile. [1]

2. **The hero form is visually dense.** Several controls sit over a photographic background, with labels and dropdowns competing for attention. The service dropdown contains many near-duplicate choices (for example, household shifting, BHK variants, bike/two-wheeler variants), increasing choice burden. A short progressive form would be easier to scan and complete. [1]

3. **Conversion paths are not fully consistent.** The page’s regional phone number is North Goa-specific, while the linked `/search` quote page switches to Delhi India and a different helpline. That context shift can reduce confidence after clicking “Request a Quote.” [2]

4. **Trust claims are plentiful but not always immediately verifiable.** Certifications and ratings are presented as claims or badges, while supporting documents, review context, service-area proof, and a clear explanation of what happens after submission are not prominent near the first CTA. The persistent WhatsApp label also contains the typo “10 mints.” [1]

## Original ideas Perfect Packways could adapt

1. **Use a two-step “instant estimate” flow.** Ask only route, move type, and approximate move date first. Then reveal optional inventory details and contact fields with a clear progress indicator. This preserves qualification while reducing above-the-fold form density.

2. **Create a service chooser with outcome-led cards.** Instead of a long dropdown, offer cards such as “Home move,” “Vehicle move,” “Office move,” and “Storage,” each with a one-line scope description and a route to a tailored quote. Keep specialist services available under “More services.”

3. **Pair each trust signal with proof and relevance.** Near the form, show a compact “Why trust us” strip with a verifiable certificate/review link, local service coverage, insurance terms, and a response-time promise. Keep the detail one click away rather than stacking many badges in the page body.

4. **Make the post-submit expectation explicit.** State what happens after a quote request (“A coordinator calls within X minutes,” “survey options,” and “no-obligation estimate”), and keep the same city/branch identity across the landing page, quote flow, phone number, WhatsApp handoff, and confirmation message.
